How Much Home Loan Can I Get on My Salary? (2026 Eligibility Guide)

Banks decide your home loan limit from FOIR, EMI capacity and LTV — not from your CTC alone. Here is exactly how the maths works, with a salary-wise eligibility table.

"How much home loan can I get on my salary?" is the first question every buyer asks — and banks answer it with a formula, not a gut feel. Two numbers decide your limit: how much EMI your salary can carry (FOIR) and how much of the property price the lender will finance (LTV). This guide shows the exact arithmetic, a salary-wise eligibility table, and every factor that moves the number up or down.

The eligibility formula banks actually use

Most Indian banks cap total EMIs — existing plus the new home loan — at 50–55% of net monthly income. This ratio is called FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio). The working formula:

Eligible EMI = (FOIR% × net monthly income) − existing EMIs
Eligible loan = EMI × [1 − (1+r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r  (present value of that EMI at your rate and tenure)

At 8.5% for 20 years, every ₹10,000 of monthly EMI capacity supports roughly ₹11.5 lakh of loan. The bank then applies the second cap: LTV, typically 75–90% of the property value (90% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for ₹30–75 lakh, 75% above ₹75 lakh, per RBI norms). Your sanction is the lower of the FOIR number and the LTV number.

So for the most-asked case — how much home loan can I get on a ₹50,000 salary — the answer is roughly ₹28–29 lakh on a 20-year tenure, or about ₹25 lakh if you already pay a ₹5,000 EMI elsewhere. A co-applicant's income adds directly to the pool: two ₹50,000 salaries can support roughly ₹57–58 lakh.

What increases (or shrinks) your eligible amount

  • Existing EMIs subtract rupee-for-rupee from your EMI capacity — a ₹10,000 car EMI cuts eligibility by about ₹11.5 lakh.
  • Tenure: stretching 20 → 30 years adds roughly 18% to the eligible loan, at the cost of substantially more total interest.
  • Credit score: above 750 keeps you at the best FOIR and rate; below 700 many lenders trim eligibility or load the rate by 0.1–0.5%.
  • Age: banks want the loan to end by retirement (58–65). A 45-year-old borrower may be forced into a shorter tenure, lowering eligibility.
  • Income proof: salary slips get the cleanest treatment; business income is averaged over 2–3 years and sometimes haircut 10–20%.
  • LTV: a bigger down payment removes the property-value cap — eligibility then depends purely on income.

A worked example: ₹60,000 salary, one bike EMI

Rahul nets ₹60,000/month and pays a ₹4,000 bike EMI. FOIR capacity = 50% × 60,000 = ₹30,000; minus the bike EMI leaves ₹26,000 for the home loan EMI. At 8.5% over 20 years that is about ₹29.9 lakh. He is buying a ₹45 lakh flat, so 80% LTV allows ₹36 lakh — income is the binding constraint, and his realistic sanction is ₹29–30 lakh, with ₹15 lakh (33%) down payment. Clearing the bike loan first would lift eligibility to nearly ₹34.6 lakh.

Frequently asked questions

How much home loan can I get on a ₹50,000 salary?

About ₹28–29 lakh on a 20-year tenure at 8.5% with no other EMIs (50% FOIR). Existing EMIs reduce this rupee-for-rupee; a 30-year tenure stretches it to roughly ₹34 lakh.

How much home loan can I get on a ₹1 lakh salary?

Roughly ₹57–58 lakh over 20 years, or about ₹68 lakh over 30 years, assuming no existing EMIs and a credit score above 750.

Is home loan eligibility based on gross or net salary?

Net (take-home) salary. Banks compute FOIR on the amount that actually reaches your account after statutory deductions.

Can I increase my eligibility with a co-applicant?

Yes — a spouse’s or parent’s income adds to the pool and can lift the eligible loan by 50–100% depending on their income.

Why did the bank sanction less than the calculator shows?

Banks apply their own FOIR (45–55%), LTV caps, age at loan maturity and internal policy overlays. Treat any calculator — including ours — as a pre-qualification estimate.
