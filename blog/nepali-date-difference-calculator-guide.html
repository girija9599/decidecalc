Why Nepali calendar arithmetic is tricky

The Bikram Sambat (BS) calendar, Nepal's official calendar, has two quirks that make simple subtraction impossible:

  • Variable month lengths: While most months have 30 or 31 days, some like Asar have 32 days. This means you can't use fixed offsets for month calculations.
  • Year offset: BS year 2000 began on April 14, 1943 AD—not January 1. This requires proper calendar data rather than a simple +567-year offset.

Our calculator uses a library containing actual Nepali calendar data (covering BS 2000–2090), ensuring accurate conversions and date differences for any date in that range.

How it works

Calendar data

The calculator embeds a library with complete Nepali calendar data for BS years 2000–2090. This includes month lengths for each year, allowing accurate forward/backward date arithmetic.

Duration algorithm

When calculating the difference between two dates:

  1. Convert both dates to a common scale (AD for internal calculation)
  2. Subtract days first; if negative, borrow from the previous month using its actual length
  3. Subtract months; if negative, borrow from the previous year
  4. Output the final Y/M/D breakdown
