Why Nepali Date Calculations Are Hard

The Bikram Sambat (BS) calendar — Nepal's official calendar — doesn't work like the Gregorian calendar you use every day:

  • Variable month lengths: Baisakh (30/31), Jestha (31/32), Asar (31/32), Shrawan (31/32), Bhadra (31/32), Aswin (30), Kartik (30), Mangsir (30), Poush (29/30), Magh (30), Falgun (29/30), Chaitra (30/31). Some months have 32 days!
  • Year offset: BS 2000 started on April 14, 1943 AD — not January 1. A simple +567-year offset fails for most dates.
  • No fixed leap-year rule: Leap days shift based on astronomical observations, not a formula.

That's why subtracting two BS dates manually (or with a simple +567 offset) gives wrong results. Our calculator uses the actual BS calendar data (BS 2000–2090) — month-by-month day counts from the official calendar — so every calculation is calendar-accurate.

How It Works (Under the Hood)

Real Calendar Data, Not Approximations

The calculator embeds the nepali-date-converter library with complete calendar data for BS 2000–2090. This includes the exact day count for every month of every year — no 30-day assumptions, no fixed offsets.

Duration Algorithm

  1. Convert both dates to a common internal representation
  2. Subtract days first; if negative, borrow from the previous month using that month's actual length
  3. Subtract months; if negative, borrow 12 months from the year
  4. Output the final Y/M/D breakdown

This mirrors how banks in Nepal calculate loan tenure — by calendar months, not 30-day chunks.

What is the Nepali BS calendar?

The Bikram Sambat (BS) is Nepal's official calendar. BS year 2000 began on April 14, 1943 AD. Unlike the Gregorian calendar, Nepali months have variable lengths (e.g., Asar has 32 days, Poush has 29 days). This makes manual date arithmetic complex and error-prone.

How does this calculator handle BS↔AD conversion?

It uses a library with actual Nepali calendar data (BS 2000–2090), not approximate offsets. This ensures accurate conversion for any date in that range. The engine uses real month-by-month day counts from the official calendar.

What loan tenure modes are available?

Three modes: Elapsed (time since loan started), Remaining (time left until loan ends), and End Date (calculate maturity date from start date and tenure). The calculator properly borrows months/days when subtracting, accounting for variable month lengths.
