How to Reduce Your Home Loan EMI: 7 Practical Ways (India, 2026)

Home loan EMIs are one of the few large expenses you have real control over. Here are seven ways to bring that number down, starting today.

A ₹9,847 EMI on a ₹10 lakh, 15-year home loan at 8.5% doesn't feel like much on its own — until you add a car loan, a credit card and rising costs on top of it. If your EMIs are eating into your monthly budget, the good news is that home loan EMIs are one of the few large expenses you have real control over. Here are seven ways to bring that number down, starting today.

1. Make a larger down payment

Every rupee you pay upfront is a rupee you never pay interest on. Pushing your down payment from 10% to 20% of the property value on a ₹50 lakh home, for instance, cuts roughly ₹5 lakh off your principal — which lowers your EMI for the entire tenure, not just the first year.

2. Choose a shorter tenure if you can afford the higher EMI

A shorter tenure means a higher monthly EMI but dramatically less interest paid overall, since less time means less compounding. If your income comfortably covers a higher EMI, a 15-year loan instead of a 20-year loan on the same amount can save well over ₹10 lakh in total interest.

3. Prepay even one extra EMI a year

This is the single most effective lever for most borrowers. Because home loans use reducing-balance interest, an extra payment goes almost entirely toward principal. One additional EMI per year on a typical 20-year loan can shorten the tenure by roughly 3–4 years.

4. Compare rates across lenders — even 0.25% matters

On a ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan, dropping the rate from 9% to 8.75% saves close to ₹2 lakh in total interest. Most lenders will match a competing offer if you ask, especially if your repayment record is clean and your credit score is above 750.

5. Refinance (balance transfer) if your current rate is stale

Loans taken 3–5 years ago are often sitting well above current market rates. A balance transfer to a new lender can lower your rate meaningfully, though it's only worth it once you account for processing and transfer fees — model both scenarios before switching. 6. Switch from fixed to floating (or vice versa) at the right point in the cycle

Floating rates track the repo rate and tend to be cheaper over a full cycle, but fixed rates offer certainty. In a falling-rate environment, floating usually wins; in a rising one, a fixed rate can protect your budget.

7. Keep your EMI under 40% of take-home pay

This isn't a way to reduce an existing EMI, but it's the single best way to avoid an unaffordable one in the first place. Lenders use a similar threshold when approving loans — use it as your own ceiling before you sign. Not sure how much loan your salary can safely carry? Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to reduce my home loan EMI?

Prepaying even one extra EMI a year has the biggest impact for most borrowers, since it goes almost entirely toward principal under reducing-balance interest.

Is it better to reduce EMI or reduce tenure when I prepay?

Reducing tenure (keeping the EMI the same) usually saves more total interest than reducing the EMI and keeping the tenure the same — run both scenarios in the EMI calculator to compare.

Does refinancing always save money?

Not always — factor in processing fees and transfer charges. It's usually worth it if the rate difference is 0.5% or more and you have significant tenure remaining.
