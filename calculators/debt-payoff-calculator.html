How the payoff calculation works

This calculator works backwards from a goal: you set the number of months, and it finds the fixed monthly payment that zeroes your balance exactly in that time, using the standard reducing-balance formula:

Formula: M = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = current balance, r = monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = your chosen months.

Worked example: ₹1,50,000 credit-card balance at 40%

Indian credit-card revolving APRs run 36–42%, so take a ₹1,50,000 balance at 40%. To clear it in 24 months you'd pay ₹9,178/month — ₹2,20,276 total, of which ₹70,276 is pure interest. Stretch it to 36 months and the payment drops to ₹7,217, but interest climbs to ₹1,09,795. Tighten to 18 months and it's ₹11,216/month with ₹51,887 interest. Now compare with the card's own minimum-payment schedule (typically 5% of the bill): at that rate the balance can linger 5+ years and cost ₹1.5 lakh+ in interest — the single most expensive way to carry a balance in India. That gap is what this calculator exists to show.

Choosing your payoff timeline

The right "months" value is the largest payment your budget genuinely sustains, not the smallest number that feels safe. Two principles help. First, interest cost is convex in time: at 40%, every extra year costs roughly ₹30,000–₹40,000 on this balance, so cutting 36 → 24 months saves ₹40,000 while raising the EMI by only ₹2,000. Second, the payment must survive bad months — if ₹9,178 fails the month your car needs repairs, choose 30 months at ₹8,067 and actually stay consistent rather than defaulting and restarting the penalty cycle. If you have multiple debts, this tool handles one balance at a time; the Debt Payoff Planner below compares avalanche and snowball ordering across all of them, and a balance transfer to a 12–14% personal loan may beat both if your card rate is above 36%.

Tips to pay off debt faster

  • Convert, don't revolve: most Indian banks convert a card balance into 12–24 EMIs at 13–18% on request — a 20-point rate cut for one phone call.
  • Aim payments at statement dates, not due dates: interest accrues daily on revolving balances; paying mid-cycle trims the average daily balance.
  • Throw windfalls at principal: a ₹40,000 bonus prepaid on this example removes roughly ₹30,000 of future interest at 40%.
  • Stop the drip: pause cards (or freeze them in a drawer) while paying off — new spends restart the cycle and make the timeline fictional.
  • Check the minimum-payment trap: read your statement's "minimum due" vs "total due" — paying the former is how a ₹1.5 lakh balance becomes a decade of debt.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to pay off ₹1 lakh of credit-card debt?

Depends entirely on the payment, not the card. At a typical 5% minimum (~₹5,000 initially), effectively 5+ years and ₹50,000+ in interest. At a fixed ₹10,000/month, about 11 months with ₹6,000–₹7,000 interest. The "Months" slider above shows the exact trade-off for your balance.

What is the minimum payment trap on credit cards?

Card minimums (usually 5% of the bill or a floor amount) are engineered to keep you revolving: nearly all of an early minimum payment goes to interest at 36–42% APR, and the rest of your unpaid bill keeps accruing. Paying only minimums on ₹1,50,000 can cost over ₹1 lakh in interest before the balance clears. Always fix a real payoff payment instead.

Is it better to pay off debt or invest while in debt?

Compare rates. Debt at 36–42% (cards) should be cleared before almost any investing — no mutual fund reliably beats 40%. Debt at 9% (home loan) can coexist with SIP investing at an expected 11–12%. The crossover sits roughly where the debt rate exceeds ~10–12%; use the EMI vs SIP comparison tool for the exact math on your numbers.

Does paying off debt improve my credit score?

Yes — utilisation (balance ÷ limit) is a major CIBIL factor, so bringing a card balance down lifts the score within 1–2 billing cycles. Keep the card open and use it lightly afterwards: closing it can shorten your credit history and nudge the score down. Consistent on-time payments during the payoff compound the benefit.

Should I use a balance transfer or a debt consolidation loan?

Both replace your rate, so compare the all-in costs: balance transfers offer 0–1% for 3–6 months then jump to card rates — good only if you can clear within the window. A 12–14% personal loan gives a fixed EMI over 24–48 months — better for larger balances needing structure. On a ₹1,50,000 balance, consolidation at 13% over 24 months costs about ₹7,133/month and ₹21,200 interest versus ₹70,000+ at card rates.

What if I can't afford any meaningful payoff payment?

Contact the lender before missing anything — banks restructure genuinely stressed accounts into EMI plans, tenure extensions or one-time settlements, and RBI's recovery norms protect borrowers who engage in good faith. Also check whether spending can be cut or income supplemented: at 40% interest, every month of delay costs ₹5,000 on a ₹1,50,000 balance — the most expensive month of anyone's financial year.
