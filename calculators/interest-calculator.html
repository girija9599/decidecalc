Simple vs compound interest: the full picture

Interest is the rent on money — earned on savings, paid on loans. Every Indian product prices it one of two ways:

Simple interest: SI = P × r × t (interest only on the original principal). Compound interest: A = P × (1 + r/n)nt — interest itself earns interest, accelerating growth.

Worked example: ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 5 years, both ways

Simple interest: ₹1,00,000 × 8% × 5 = ₹40,000 — maturity ₹1,40,000. Compounded annually at the same 8%: ₹1,00,000 × (1.08)⁵ = ₹1,46,933 — ₹6,933 more. Compound quarterly (how many Indian FDs actually compound) and it reaches ₹1,48,594. The gap widens dramatically with time: at 20 years, the same 8% compounding quarterly yields ₹3,87,000 versus ₹2,60,000 simple — the difference is ₹1,27,000, entirely from compounding.

Where each type shows up in India

Compound products: bank FDs and RDs (usually quarterly compounding), PPF (annual, currently 7.1%), NSC, KVP (10 years 8 months to double), savings accounts (daily balance, quarterly payout), EPF, and of course mutual funds. Simple-interest products: most car and personal loans quote annual reducing-balance rates — which behave like compounding in reverse, because each EMI kills part of the principal that would otherwise compound. The rule of thumb: compounding favours the saver, and kills the borrower. That's why a 40% credit-card rate compounded monthly (3.33%/month) turns ₹1.5 lakh into ₹2.2 lakh in two years, and why the Rule of 72 — divide 72 by the rate to estimate doubling time — is worth memorising: at 7.1% (PPF), your money doubles in ~10.1 years.

Tips to make interest work for you

  • Check the compounding frequency, not just the rate: two 8% FDs differ if one compounds quarterly and the other annually — ₹1,661 extra per lakh per 5 years.
  • Start early, not big: ₹5,000/month at 12% for 25 years beats ₹15,000/month for 12 years — compounding rewards duration over size.
  • Let FD interest ride: cumulative FDs reinvest interest and compound; non-cumulative payouts that you spend do not.
  • Attack high-rate debt first: interest compounding against you at 36–42% (credit cards) outruns any investment compounding for you.
  • Remember tax: FD interest is taxed at slab yearly, so your real compound rate is post-tax — a 30%-slab investor earns effectively ~5.6% on an 8% FD.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal every year (₹1,00,000 at 8% earns ₹8,000 every year). Compound interest is calculated on principal plus accumulated interest, so the second year earns more than the first. Over 5 years at 8%, that gap is ₹6,933 per lakh — over 20 years, over ₹1.2 lakh.

Do Indian bank FDs use simple or compound interest?

Compound — most banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI) compound FD interest quarterly, meaning an advertised 8% actually yields about 8.24% effective annually. Interest is payable monthly/quarterly (non-cumulative, no compounding benefit) or at maturity (cumulative, fully compounded). Check which one you're choosing on the FD application form.

What is the Rule of 72?

A mental-math shortcut: divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years money takes to double. At 7.1% (PPF), 72 ÷ 7.1 ≈ 10 years. At 12% (equity assumption), 6 years. At 9% (home loan against you), your lender's money doubles every 8 years if you never prepay — which is the rule's other, sobering use.

Is FD interest taxable in India?

Yes — fully, at your income-tax slab, every year (unlike equity mutual funds, taxed only on redemption). Banks deduct 10% TDS if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for seniors), but the real tax at slab rates is higher. A 30%-slab investor earns effectively ~5.6% post-tax on an 8% FD.

Which is better — a higher simple rate or lower compound rate?

Convert to effective annual yield first. A 7.5% rate compounded quarterly yields 7.71% effective; a 7.6% simple rate yields 7.6%. For multi-year products the compounded option usually wins, but on loans, simple/reducing-balance quoting is the borrower-friendlier convention — that's why flat-rate loan quotes (which sound simple but compound harshly) should always be converted before comparing.

How does compounding work against me on loans?

Exactly the same math, reversed: unpaid credit-card balances accrue interest daily at 36–42% APR, and that interest itself accrues interest. ₹1.5 lakh revolving at 40% becomes ₹2.2 lakh in 24 months if untouched. The defence is symmetric: EMIs that kill principal monthly prevent compounding from running — the faster the principal falls, the less interest can compound.
