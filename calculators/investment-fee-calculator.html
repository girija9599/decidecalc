Why a small expense ratio can matter

Fees are charged repeatedly on invested assets, so their impact compounds over time. A fund with a low stated cost is not automatically the right choice: consider benchmark, risk, diversification, tracking difference, taxes and your goal. This calculator isolates the fee effect so you can make a clearer comparison.

Worked example: what a 1% fee costs over 25 years

Take a ₹5,00,000 lump sum plus a ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 25 years at a 12% gross return. With zero fees the portfolio reaches about ₹2.75 crore. Apply a typical actively-managed fund's 1.2% expense ratio and it lands near ₹2.19 crore — the fee drag is roughly ₹56 lakh. An index fund charging 0.5% instead grows to about ₹2.50 crore (₹25 lakh drag). Choosing the cheaper structure in this example keeps around ₹31 lakh more in your pocket — for taking nearly the same market risk.

Expense ratios in India: what is normal

SEBI caps total expense ratios (TER) by fund size: roughly up to 2.25% for the smallest equity funds, sliding to ~1.05% for the largest, and lower for debt funds. In practice, most direct-plan index funds charge 0.1–0.5%, direct actively-managed funds 0.6–1.2%, and regular plans (which pay distributor commissions) sit 0.5–1% above their direct equivalents. Over 25 years on the example above, that commission difference alone compounds into tens of lakhs — which is why direct plans through platforms, plus low-cost index funds for the core of a portfolio, are the standard low-fee advice in India. Test your own numbers above: even a 0.3% reduction is worth running.

Not investment advice: market returns are uncertain and an expense ratio can change. Review the current scheme document and compare like-for-like funds before investing.

Frequently asked questions

What is an expense ratio?

It is an annual operating cost charged by a fund as a percentage of assets. The published NAV usually already reflects it, but it still reduces the return investors receive.

Does a lower expense ratio guarantee better returns?

No. Cost is one input. Compare the fund mandate, risk, benchmark, consistency, taxes and suitability for your goal.

Are taxes included here?

No. This is a simplified pre-tax projection designed to show fee drag. Taxes, exit loads and transaction costs can materially change a real result.

What is the difference between direct and regular plans?

Regular plans pay a distributor commission out of the fund's assets, so their expense ratio runs 0.5–1% higher than the identical direct plan. You get the same portfolio and the same returns before fees — the only difference is who you bought through. Buying direct (via fund-house websites or platforms) is the single biggest fee decision most Indian investors can make.

What expense ratio is reasonable in India?

For index funds, 0.1–0.5% is the healthy range. For actively-managed direct equity funds, up to about 1% is defensible if the fund consistently beats its benchmark after fees. Above 1.5% (common in regular plans), the odds of the fund out-earning its fee drag shrink sharply — run the comparison above with both ratios and let the numbers decide.
