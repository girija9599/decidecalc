How any loan payment is calculated

Home loans, car loans, personal loans, gold loans — every reducing-balance loan in India uses one formula, which is what makes this calculator universal:

Formula: M = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = principal, r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of monthly instalments.

Worked example: ₹5,00,000 loan at 11% for 4 years

With P = ₹5,00,000, r = 0.009167 and n = 48, the monthly payment is ₹12,923 — total repayment ₹6,20,293, of which ₹1,20,293 is interest. If the same loan came from an NBFC at 12.5%, the payment rises to ₹13,290 and total interest to ₹1,37,920 — ₹17,627 more for the same ₹5 lakh. That is what two percentage points of rate actually cost, and why comparing lenders on the annual reducing rate (not the EMI) is the highest-value two minutes in borrowing.

Typical loan rates in India (2026)

Because this tool works for any loan type, here's the rate landscape you should be testing against: home loans 8.25–9.5% (the cheapest credit in the system, RBI repo-linked); loan against property 9–12%; gold loans 9–14%; new-car loans 8.5–11%; education loans 8.25–12%; used-car loans 12–16%; personal loans 10.5–16% at banks, 16–24% at NBFCs/fintechs; credit-card EMIs 13–24%; two-wheeler loans 9–18%. If a quote lands far above its band, either your credit profile is pricing it there or the lender is quoting a flat rate — a "9% flat" quote equals roughly 16–17% reducing, the classic two-wheeler trap. Enter the quoted numbers above and check the total interest line: it is the single number that makes loan offers comparable.

How amortization works inside every payment

Each EMI splits into interest and principal, and the split shifts over time: in month 1 of the ₹5 lakh example, ₹4,583 of your ₹12,923 is interest and only ₹8,340 is principal; by the final year the ratio has flipped almost entirely. This is why prepaying early saves far more interest than prepaying late — money prepaid in year 1 stops a full tenure's worth of interest accrual on that amount. It also explains the counterintuitive rule that a shorter tenure is almost always cheaper overall, even at a painful EMI: the same ₹5 lakh at 11% for 3 years costs ₹16,369/month but only ₹89,297 in total interest — ₹30,996 less than the 4-year version.

Frequently asked questions

How do I verify my bank's EMI quote is correct?

Enter the principal, the annual reducing-balance rate and the exact tenure (in months) above, and compare the EMI. Small differences (under 0.5%) come from rounding or day-count conventions; large differences usually mean the quoted rate is flat instead of reducing, or fees are financed into the loan. Ask the lender to state the annual reducing rate and the total repayment in writing.

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance?

Reducing balance (this calculator) charges interest only on the outstanding principal each month, so interest shrinks as you repay. Flat rate charges interest on the original principal for the whole tenure. A flat rate of 10% equals roughly 17–18% reducing balance — always convert before comparing offers, especially on two-wheeler and consumer-durable loans.

Why is the same loan amount more expensive at an NBFC?

NBFCs lend to riskier borrower segments and fund themselves slightly more expensively than banks, so their rates run 1–4% higher. They compensate with faster approvals and looser documentation. On a ₹5 lakh loan, a 12.5% NBFC rate versus an 11% bank rate costs about ₹17,600 extra over 4 years — sometimes worth it for speed, but know the price.

Does prepaying a loan really save interest?

Yes — most when done early. Prepaying ₹1 lakh in year 1 of the ₹5 lakh/11%/4-year example saves roughly ₹47,000 in interest over the remaining tenure; the same prepayment in year 3 saves only about ₹13,000. Check your agreement for prepayment penalties (RBI bars them on floating-rate individual loans) and always opt to reduce tenure rather than EMI when prepaying.

Is a longer tenure with small EMIs ever smart?

Only as cash-flow insurance, not as a plan. Taking the 5-year EMI (₹10,871 on this example) while voluntarily prepaying the 4-year amount whenever possible gives you flexibility: in tight months you pay the legal minimum, in good months you cut interest. But simply choosing the longest tenure and paying exactly it is the most expensive way to borrow.

What costs does this calculator not include?

Processing fees (0.4–2.5% typical), GST on fees and on interest for consumer loans, insurance premiums bundled into the loan, documentation charges, and any late/bounce penalties. Add financed fees to the loan amount manually if you want the all-in EMI — and compare lenders on total repayment, not just the rate.
