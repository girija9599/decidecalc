How TV EMI is calculated

A 55-inch 4K TV in India spans ₹35,000 (Xiaomi/Thomson) to ₹1,00,000+ (Sony/Samsung/LG OLED) — and TV financing uses the same reducing-balance instalment formula as every other EMI:

Formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = amount financed (price − down payment − exchange value), r = monthly interest rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of monthly instalments.

Worked example: ₹60,000 TV at 16% for 12 months

With P = ₹60,000, r = 0.013333 and n = 12, the EMI is ₹5,444 per month — ₹65,326 repaid in total, of which ₹5,326 is interest. On a 6-month no-cost EMI (0%), the same TV is ₹10,000/month with zero interest; over 24 months at 16%, the interest balloons to ₹10,651 — you'd have paid nearly ₹71,000 for a ₹60,000 TV that has already dropped a size class in price. The pattern is consistent: for TVs, timing the purchase to a sale beats choosing a longer tenure every time.

TV financing in India: where the deals are

TVs are the classic Indian festive-season purchase, and the financing market reflects that. No-cost EMI windows (6–12 months) on Sony, Samsung, LG and Xiaomi TVs appear reliably during Diwali, Flipkart Big Billion Days and Amazon Great Indian Festival — bank cards from HDFC, ICICI, Axis and SBI dominate these offers. Zero-down-payment NBFC schemes at Croma, Vijay Sales, Reliance Digital and local dealers (Bajaj Finserv, HomeCredit) let you walk out with a TV paying nothing upfront — convenient, but check the interest beyond the no-cost window, and note that 18% GST applies on the interest component. Exchange value matters more for TVs than most gadgets: an old working TV fetches ₹3,000–₹15,000 in exchange depending on size and condition, directly shrinking the financed amount. Two traps to avoid: BNPL "pay in 3" apps at checkout that quietly carry 18–24% after the free window, and extended warranties (₹3,000–₹8,000 on a large TV) that get bundled into the financed amount by default — decide them separately.

Tips before you finance a TV

  • Buy in the sale window: last year's flagship at Diwali pricing with no-cost EMI beats this year's mid-range on 24-month credit.
  • Exchange the old TV: ₹3,000–₹15,000 off the principal is the cheapest "down payment" you'll get.
  • Keep tenure ≤ 12 months: TV prices fall constantly — paying interest into year two means financing depreciation.
  • Check panel warranty vs extended warranty: most brands include 1 year; panel-only extended coverage can often be added later if needed.
  • Budget the extras separately: wall mount (₹500–₹1,500), installation, and a UPS/stabilizer in areas with voltage swings — don't let them ride silently into the EMI.

Frequently asked questions

How much EMI for a ₹50,000 TV?

At 16% over 12 months, about ₹4,537/month (₹54,439 total). On a 6-month no-cost EMI, ₹8,334/month with zero interest; over 24 months at 16%, roughly ₹2,445/month but ₹8,690 in total interest. Use the sliders above for your exact numbers.

Is buying a TV on EMI a good idea?

On a genuine no-cost plan during a sale, yes — you keep your savings intact and pay the sticker price in instalments. On regular 16–24% interest, no: a ₹60,000 TV financed over 24 months costs ₹10,000+ extra while the same TV's price keeps falling. If there's no no-cost window, waiting 2–3 months to save up is almost always cheaper.

What is zero down payment TV financing?

Stores and NBFCs (Bajaj Finserv, HomeCredit, IDFC First) let you take a TV home with no upfront money, converting the full price into EMIs — often on Aadhaar + PAN KYC alone. The catch: beyond the advertised no-cost window, interest runs 16–28%, and processing fees of ₹200–₹500 may apply. It's convenient for genuine cash-flow crunches, expensive as a habit.

Does exchange value reduce my TV EMI?

Yes — the exchange credit is subtracted from the TV's price before financing, so it directly shrinks the principal and therefore every EMI. An old 32-inch working TV typically fetches ₹3,000–₹6,000; large 43–55-inch LED/LCD sets ₹8,000–₹15,000. Note some stores reduce the exchange bonus if you choose no-cost EMI, so compare the final invoice both ways.

What happens if I miss a TV EMI payment?

The auto-debit bounces (₹300–₹500 bank charge), the lender adds a late fee of 1.5–2% per month on the overdue amount, and after 30+ days the miss hits your CIBIL report. On NBFC consumer loans, repeated misses can lead to recovery agent calls and acceleration of the full balance. If a payment will fail, inform the lender in advance to request a revised debit date.

Can I prepay or foreclose a TV EMI early?

Yes — most lenders allow foreclosure after 3–6 EMIs with a charge of 2–5% of the outstanding principal plus GST. On no-cost plans, early closure can sometimes claw back the interest subsidy, making it cheaper to just continue — ask for both figures in writing before deciding. Regular high-interest plans are always worth foreclosing early.
