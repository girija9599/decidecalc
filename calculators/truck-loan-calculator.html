How truck loan EMI is calculated

Commercial vehicle loans in India — from ₹8 lakh mini-trucks to ₹55 lakh multi-axle rigs — use the same reducing-balance formula as any vehicle loan, priced for a business asset:

Formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = amount financed (chassis + body + extras − down payment), r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = months.

Worked example: ₹25,00,000 truck with ₹5,00,000 down at 10.5%

An intermediate or heavy commercial vehicle on-road at ₹25,00,000 with a 20% down payment leaves P = ₹20,00,000. At 10.5% for 60 months, the EMI is ₹42,988 — total repayment ₹25,79,268, of which ₹5,79,268 is interest. Stretch to 72 months and the EMI eases to ₹37,558 but interest climbs to ₹7,04,172. The business test that matters: most operators clear 12–18 trips a month, and the EMI plus fuel, tolls, driver salary and maintenance should stay under about 70% of realistic monthly revenue — a truck bought on hope of full utilisation is the most common default story in CV finance.

How commercial vehicle financing works in India

CV lending is dominated by specialist NBFCs — Sundaram Finance, Cholamandalam, Mahindra Finance, Shriram, Tata Capital — who understand truck economics better than banks and price accordingly: 10.5–14% per year for established operators with track record, 14–18% for first-time owners. The structure differs from car loans in three ways. Funding is unusual: lenders may finance 100% of the chassis but treat body fabrication (₹1.5–₹8 lakh for tipper/tanker bodies) as a separate financed line — enter it under "Body & extras %" above. Moratoriums exist: many lenders offer a 1–3 month EMI holiday at the start (useful for getting the vehicle on the road), but interest accrues throughout. The vehicle is the business: comprehensive insurance is mandatory for the full tenure, the RTO hypothecation sits with the lender, and national permits/fitness certificates must stay current. SME borrowers should also compare a balance transfer after 2–3 years of clean repayment — CV refinancing between NBFCs is common and can cut 1–2% off the rate.

Tips before financing a truck

  • Run the utilisation math first: model EMI + fuel + tolls + salary + maintenance against realistic trip income at 60–70% utilisation, not 100%.
  • Compare specialist NBFCs and banks: Sundaram, Chola and Mahindra quote differently for the same profile; even 1% is ₹1.2 lakh on this example.
  • Keep 15–20% margin money: zero-down CV loans exist but push the EMI into dangerous territory during lean months.
  • Check the moratorium's true cost: interest accrues during the holiday — ask for the EMI with and without it.
  • Budget the first year's insurance and permits upfront: comprehensive cover plus fitness and permits can add ₹80,000–₹1,50,000 at signing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the EMI for a ₹25 lakh truck?

With a ₹5 lakh down payment (financing ₹20 lakh) at 10.5%: about ₹42,988/month over 5 years, ₹51,207/month over 4 years, or ₹37,558/month over 6 years. Total interest ranges from ₹4.58 lakh (4 yr) to ₹7.04 lakh (6 yr). Use the sliders above for your exact vehicle and terms.

What credit score or track record do I need for a CV loan?

Established operators with 2–3 years of clean repayment history and a CIBIL score above 700 get the best rates (10.5–12%). First-time owners face 14–18%, bigger down payments (30–50%), or a guarantor. Many lenders also weigh fleet composition, route contracts and existing-vehicle performance — a documented trip ledger strengthens the application more than a credit card limit does.

Can I finance 100% of a truck's cost?

Some lenders advertise 100% chassis funding, but realistic total funding — chassis plus body, insurance and registration — usually lands at 85–95% for established operators and 60–75% for first-timers. The margin money requirement exists to keep the EMI within survivable utilisation: a truck financed to the brim leaves no buffer for the first slow month.

What is a moratorium period on a truck loan?

A grace window (1–3 months typical) before the first EMI, meant to let the vehicle start earning. Interest accrues at the full rate during it, so a 2-month moratorium on a ₹20 lakh loan adds roughly ₹35,000 of interest across the tenure. Take it only if the vehicle genuinely needs the ramp-up time; otherwise start EMIs immediately.

What happens if the truck earning drops and I miss EMIs?

CV lenders act fast because the vehicle is the collateral: late fees of 2–3% monthly, recovery-agent escalation within 60–90 days, and repossession of the vehicle — which destroys both the business and the credit file. If utilisation drops, engage the lender before defaulting: most NBFCs will restructure the tenure, offer a standstill month, or refinance rather than repossess a paying-in-full-history customer.

Can I refinance or transfer my truck loan to a cheaper lender?

Yes — balance transfer is well established in CV finance. After 12+ EMIs of clean payment, competitors (including banks entering the segment) will refinance the outstanding at 1–2% lower, charging a processing fee and RTO hypothecation-change costs. On a ₹15 lakh outstanding, a 1.5% saving is worth about ₹45,000 over the remaining tenure — worth the paperwork every time.
