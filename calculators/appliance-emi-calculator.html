How appliance EMI is calculated

Refrigerators (₹15,000–₹80,000), washing machines (₹15,000–₹60,000), air-conditioners (₹30,000–₹75,000) and microwaves all run through the same reducing-balance instalment formula when financed:

Formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = amount financed (price − down payment − exchange value), r = monthly interest rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of monthly instalments.

Worked example: ₹45,000 refrigerator at 16% for 12 months

With P = ₹45,000, r = 0.013333 and n = 12, the EMI is ₹4,083 per month — ₹48,995 repaid in total, ₹3,995 of it interest. On a 10-month no-cost EMI (0%), the same fridge is ₹4,500/month with no interest. Now compare that ₹3,995 interest against the appliance's running economics: upgrading from a 3-star to a 5-star 340L refrigerator saves roughly ₹1,500–₹2,500 in electricity per year — meaning a slightly costlier, more efficient model bought on no-cost EMI can pay for its own price difference within the tenure.

Appliance financing in India: need-driven buying

Unlike phones or TVs, appliance purchases are often forced — a dead compressor in April or a failed washing machine with a newborn at home can't wait for a sale. India's financing system is built around that reality: zero-down-payment NBFC finance (Bajaj Finserv, HomeCredit, IDFC First) at virtually every electronics store approves in minutes on Aadhaar + PAN, with 6–10 month no-cost windows that brands subsidise heavily during summer AC season and Diwali. Credit-card EMI conversion at 12–18% is the fallback when no-cost isn't offered. Three India-specific angles matter here. First, old-appliance exchange is strongest in this category — a dead fridge or AC still fetches ₹2,000–₹8,000, and many dealers haul away the old unit free. Second, bulk purchases (full kitchen setup, or ACs for multiple rooms) often unlock better no-cost terms as a single invoice than as separate EMIs — but add up the total before financing ₹2 lakh in one go. Third, remember 18% GST on the interest component and ₹99–₹500 processing fees even on "no-cost" plans. Because appliances last 8–12 years, they're the one category where a 24-month EMI is genuinely defensible — the machine outlives the debt.

Tips before you finance an appliance

  • Efficiency first: a 5-star model on no-cost EMI often beats a 3-star model on cheaper EMI once electricity bills are counted.
  • Time optional purchases to sales: microwaves and dishwashers dip hardest during Diwali and Republic Day sales — forced purchases (dead fridge) obviously can't wait.
  • Exchange the old unit: even non-working appliances fetch ₹1,500–₹8,000 and free removal.
  • Check installation and piping: AC installs add ₹1,500–₹3,000 (copper piping extra); washing machine and fridge installs are usually free — don't finance what's quoted separately.
  • Extended warranty separately: decide cover (₹1,500–₹5,000) on its own merits rather than letting it inflate the financed amount.

Frequently asked questions

How much EMI for a ₹30,000 washing machine?

At 16% over 12 months, about ₹2,722/month (₹32,659 total). On a 9-month no-cost EMI, ₹3,334/month with zero interest. Over 24 months at 16%, roughly ₹1,547/month but ₹4,125 in total interest. Set the sliders above to your exact price.

Can I buy appliances on EMI without a credit card?

Yes — India's consumer-durable financing network is built for exactly this. Bajaj Finserv's EMI Network Card, HomeCredit and IDFC First approve in-store with Aadhaar, PAN and a bank account for NACH auto-debit, usually within 15–30 minutes. Most large stores (Croma, Vijay Sales, Reliance Digital) and even single-brand dealers accept these schemes, with 6–10 month no-cost windows on select models.

Is a 24-month EMI okay for appliances?

Yes — appliances are the exception to the short-tenure rule. A refrigerator or washing machine lasts 8–12 years, so even a 24-month plan means the machine outlives the debt. Just avoid paying regular 18–24% interest for the privilege: if there's no no-cost offer, prefer 12 months, and never stretch beyond 36 months.

What is the GST on appliance EMIs?

GST at 18% is charged on the interest component of each EMI and on processing/foreclosure fees — not on the appliance's price again (GST on the product is already in the MRP). On a typical ₹45,000 appliance EMI, the GST-on-interest adds ₹300–₹700 across the tenure. Even "no-cost" plans attract it, since the lender technically charges interest that the brand subsidises.

Should I finance an appliance or buy a cheaper one outright?

If the need is urgent (dead fridge in summer), financing a good model on a no-cost plan is smarter than buying the cheapest one outright — you get reliability without draining your buffer. If there's no no-cost offer and the need can wait 4–6 weeks, saving up wins: regular interest turns a ₹45,000 appliance into a ₹49,000 purchase. Balance urgency, offer quality, and your emergency fund.

Do appliance EMIs affect my credit score?

Yes — consumer-durable loans are reported to CIBIL like any other instalment loan. On-time EMIs build history (useful if your credit file is thin); missed ones cut your score and stay on the report for years. Multiple simultaneous appliance loans also raise your FOIR, which banks check when you apply for bigger credit like a home loan.
