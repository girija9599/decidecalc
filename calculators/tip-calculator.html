How to Calculate a Tip — the tip calculator formula

Every tip calculator app, however fancy, runs one simple formula:

Tip calculator formula: Tip = Bill × (Tip % ÷ 100)  ·  Total = Bill + Tip  ·  Per person = Total ÷ People

Example: on a $85 restaurant bill at 20%, the tip is $17 and the total is $102. Split that tip calculator result between four friends and each person pays $25.50. To do it in your head, find 10% (move the decimal one place left) and build 15%, 18%, 20% or 25% from there — the full mental-math method is in our how to calculate a tip guide.

Tipping around the world — travel tip calculator norms

Tipping culture changes the moment you cross a border, which is exactly what the travel tip calculator mode above handles. This is the tipping etiquette chart it uses (2026):

CountryTypical restaurant tipWhat travelers should know
USA18–22%Tipping is expected; many service workers rely on it as core income
Canada15–20%Similar to the US, slightly lower
Mexico10–15%Appreciated and meaningful for workers
UK10–12.5%Often already added as a "service charge" — check the bill first
Germany5–10%Rounding up is usually enough
FranceMinimalService is generally built into the price
India5–10%Informal; rounding up is common. Skip it if a service charge is already added
AustraliaNot expectedOptional, never required
JapanNot customaryCan be seen as confusing or even rude — service is already included
Brazil~10%Often already added as a service charge ("serviço")

Travelling further? The same two rules work almost everywhere: check the bill for an included service charge, and when in doubt add 10% — nobody has ever been upset by a polite tip. Full country guide: Tipping Around the World.
