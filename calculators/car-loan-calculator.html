How car loan EMI is calculated

Indian banks and NBFCs price car loans on the standard reducing-balance method — interest each month is charged only on the outstanding balance:

Formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = amount financed (on-road price + extras − down payment − exchange), r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = months.

Worked example: ₹8,00,000 car at 9% for 5 years

With P = ₹8,00,000 (after a 20% down payment of ₹1,60,000), r = 0.0075 and n = 60, the EMI is ₹16,607 per month. Over 5 years you repay ₹9,96,401 — the interest costs ₹1,96,401, about a quarter of the loan. Shorten the same loan to 3 years and the EMI rises to ₹25,439 but total interest drops to ₹1,15,811 — a saving of ₹80,590. That trade-off between monthly comfort and total cost is the single most important decision in car financing; use the sliders above to find your balance point.

What car loans actually cost in India (2026)

New-car loans from SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak and captive financiers typically run 8.5–11% per year for salaried borrowers with a CIBIL score above 750; self-employed buyers and lower scores see 11–14%. Used-car loans are costlier — 12–16% — and NBFCs charge more than banks. Beyond the rate, budget for: a processing fee of 0.4–0.5% of the loan plus GST (₹3,500–₹6,000 typical), documentation charges, and — if you skip EMIs — bounce fees plus 2–3% monthly penalties. Lenders fund 85–100% of the on-road price, but a bigger down payment directly shrinks the interest bill: every ₹1 lakh you put down on this example saves roughly ₹24,500 in interest over 5 years. Watch for "0% interest" or subvented schemes offered by manufacturers — the subsidy is real but sometimes comes with a smaller exchange bonus or mandatory accessories; compare the final on-road invoice both ways before signing. And since a car depreciates 15–20% a year, keep the tenure at 5 years or less — a 7-year loan regularly goes underwater, owing more than the car is worth.

Tips to cut your car loan cost

  • Get pre-approved before the showroom: a sanctioned offer from your bank gives you a benchmark — dealers' tied-up financiers often quote more.
  • Keep tenure ≤ 5 years: the 2-year EMI saving of a 7-year loan costs over ₹1 lakh in extra interest on this example.
  • Put down 20%+: it lowers the EMI, the interest, and the risk of negative equity when you sell.
  • Take the exchange: dealer exchange values (₹50,000–₹3,00,000) cut the principal directly — compare with selling on OLX/Spinny first.
  • Prepay one extra EMI a year: on a ₹8 lakh loan it can cut the tenure by 6–9 months.

Frequently asked questions

What is the EMI for a ₹8 lakh car loan?

At 9% interest: about ₹16,607/month over 5 years (60 EMIs), ₹20,433/month over 4 years, or ₹25,439/month over 3 years. Total interest ranges from ₹1,15,811 (3 yr) to ₹1,96,401 (5 yr). Use the sliders above for your exact price, rate and tenure.

What is a good car loan interest rate in India?

For new cars in 2026: 8.5–10% is good for salaried borrowers with a CIBIL score of 750+; 10–11% is average; above 12% deserves shopping around. Used-car rates run 12–16%, and NBFC quotes typically sit 0.5–1.5% above banks. Always compare the annual rate plus processing fee across 2–3 lenders before accepting the showroom's tied-up financier.

How much down payment should I make on a car?

Aim for at least 20% of the on-road price, plus enough to cover insurance and accessories the lender won't finance well. Lenders may offer 90–100% funding, but every rupee borrowed at 9–11% costs ~25% more over 5 years. A ₹1,60,000 down payment on a ₹8,00,000 car saves roughly ₹24,500 in interest versus zero-down.

Is a 7-year car loan a good idea?

Usually not. A 7-year term cuts the EMI on a ₹8 lakh loan from ₹16,607 to ₹12,614, but total interest jumps from ₹1,96,401 to ₹2,59,577 — and Indian cars depreciate 15–20% yearly, so for most of the tenure you owe more than the car is worth. Choose 7 years only if cash flow genuinely demands it, and prepay when you can.

Can I prepay or foreclose my car loan early?

Yes. RBI rules bar prepayment penalties on floating-rate loans to individuals, and many banks have dropped them on fixed-rate car loans too — but NBFC fixed-rate loans often charge 2–5% of the outstanding, so check your agreement. Foreclosing a ₹8 lakh loan 2 years early on this example saves roughly ₹80,000 in remaining interest.

Are 0% interest car loans actually free?

Sometimes. In a subvented scheme the manufacturer pays the financier's interest as a sales subsidy — genuinely useful. But compare the total on-road invoice with and without the scheme: dealers sometimes shrink the exchange bonus, bundle accessories, or forgo a corporate discount on "0%" deals. Also note 18% GST applies on processing fees, and zero-down variants can include hidden charges.
