How to decide: rent vs buy

This isn't just "EMI vs rent". The real comparison is net worth after the period:

  • Buy path: pay down payment + EMIs + maintenance. Your asset is the appreciated property minus the remaining loan.
  • Rent path: pay rent (rising yearly) + invest the savings (down payment + monthly EMI-rent gap) at your alternative return.
  • If property appreciation beats your alt investment rate, buying usually wins. If not, renting + investing often wins.
Rule of thumb: if annual rent < 3% of property price (rental yield), renting is typically cheaper. Above ~4%, buying looks better.

Worked example: ₹80 lakh flat vs ₹25,000 rent

Buying: ₹80,00,000 property with a ₹16,00,000 down payment leaves a ₹64,00,000 loan at 8.5% for 20 years — an EMI of ₹55,541, plus ~1% maintenance and 6% stamp duty upfront (₹4,80,000). Renting: ₹25,000/month rising 8% a year, with the down payment and the EMI-rent gap invested at 11%. After 10 years at 6% property appreciation, this calculator's net-worth comparison typically lands close to a draw — which is the honest answer: in India's metro math, neither side wins universally. Move appreciation to 8% or your stay to 15+ years and buying pulls ahead; keep rent-to-price under 3% and relocation options open, and renting + investing wins. That sensitivity is exactly why the sliders above matter more than any rule of thumb.

The costs this comparison must include

Renters in India usually ignore three buying-side costs and buyers ignore one renting-side opportunity. On the buy side: stamp duty and registration (5–7% in most states), GST on under-construction flats (5%), interiors (₹1,500–₹3,000/sq ft), maintenance, and property tax — together 10–15% on top of the base price. On the rent side: the EMI-rent gap is investable money, and in cities like Bengaluru or Gurugram, rent for an equivalent flat is often 40–60% below the EMI for owning it. HRA exemptions narrow that gap for salaried renters, while Section 24(b) (₹2 lakh interest deduction) and 80C principal benefits narrow it for buyers — both regimes' numbers, not just one side's.

A decision matrix beyond the spreadsheet

  • Upfront cost: renting needs 2–3 months' deposit; buying needs 20–30% of price in down payment + taxes + interiors before you sleep there once.
  • Liquidity: rent deposits come back in a month; property takes 6–12 months to sell and may fetch 5–10% below market in a hurry.
  • Stability: ownership ends landlord risk (eviction for sale, rent hikes); renting ends liquidity risk. India's rent agreements still favour owners — plan for moves.
  • Tax: HRA for renters vs ₹2 lakh interest deduction (Section 24b) for buyers — both shrink the effective cost meaningfully.
  • Life stage: schools, job mobility and family plans decide horizon, and horizon decides this comparison more than any interest rate.

How this calculator works

The tool simulates both paths year by year: the buy path compounds property appreciation, amortizes the loan and accumulates maintenance; the rent path grows rent at your escalation rate and invests the freed cash (down payment + monthly difference) at your alternative return. It compares net worth — property equity vs invested corpus — at the end of your horizon, not monthly payments, which is the only fair way to run this comparison. All math runs in your browser. Frequently asked questions

When does buying make more sense than renting?

When property appreciation is high (8%+), you plan to stay 7–10+ years, and the EMI is comfortably affordable. Emotional stability of ownership also matters — no landlord can ask you to leave. Run your own numbers above: the horizon slider is usually the deciding variable, not the appreciation rate.

When is renting better than buying in India?

When you may relocate within 5 years, when rent-to-price ratios are low (under 3% — common in Mumbai and Gurugram), or when you can genuinely invest the EMI-rent difference at 10%+ rather than spending it. Renting wins on flexibility and liquidity; buying wins on forced savings and stability.

Is property guaranteed to appreciate in India?

No. Indian real estate has had long flat stretches — 2014–2019 saw many metros stagnate in real terms. Location matters enormously: tier-1 city cores and job-growth corridors appreciate faster than distant suburbs with unsold inventory. The calculator lets you test conservative scenarios; plan on 5–6%, not the 12–15% of boom years.

What costs does the rent vs buy comparison usually miss?

On the buy side: stamp duty and registration (5–7%), interiors, maintenance, property tax, society corpus charges and broker fees — together often 10–15% of price. On the rent side: the opportunity return on your down payment if you didn't buy, plus HRA tax benefits that renters forget to count. Our calculator factors the big ones; interiors are best added to your own down-payment number.

Should I buy my first home to live in, even if the math says rent?

If it's your primary residence and you'll genuinely stay 10+ years, the stability, customization and forced-savings value often justify buying even when pure math is close. The math should still be checked first — the worst outcome is an EMI that caps your career choices and investment capacity, not the purchase itself.

How does the 3% rental-yield rule work?

Compare annual rent with property price: if a ₹80,00,000 flat rents for under ₹20,000/month (3% yield), renting the equivalent home is usually cheaper than owning it once maintenance and opportunity cost are counted. Above 4% yield (common in smaller cities), buying typically wins. India's metro yields of 2–3.5% are why rent-vs-buy is closer here than global rules suggest.
